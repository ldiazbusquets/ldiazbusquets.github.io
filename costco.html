01 Company overview

A membership business that happens to sell groceries.

Costco Wholesale Corporation operates a membership-based warehouse retail model that offers a limited selection of branded and private-label merchandise at low prices to both individual and business members. The strategy emphasizes high sales volume, rapid inventory turnover, and disciplined cost control, resulting in relatively low gross margins on merchandise. A significant portion of operating income is derived from annual membership fees, which provide a stable and recurring revenue stream. Costco's private-label brand, Kirkland Signature, contributes to customer loyalty and margin management while reinforcing the value proposition.

"To continually provide our members with quality goods and services at the lowest possible prices."Costco's mission

Sector and subsector

Costco operates within the Consumer Staples sector, reflecting its focus on essential goods such as food, household products, and everyday necessities that exhibit relatively stable demand across economic cycles. Within the sector it is classified in the Retail Consumer Staples subsector as a warehouse club retailer. This highlights Costco's defensive characteristics: consistent customer traffic, predictable cash flows, and resilience during periods of economic volatility.

How it reaches members

  • Warehouse clubs are the primary channel, where members buy in bulk at low margins.
  • Costco.com supplements warehouse sales with select merchandise, including large or specialty items.
  • Ancillary services such as pharmacies, optical and hearing aid centers, fuel stations, food courts, and travel drive member traffic.
  • Business-to-business sales support small and medium-sized enterprises through bulk purchasing and delivery.

The target market is value-oriented households, families, and small to medium-sized businesses, typically middle to higher income. Merchandise comes from a broad, diversified supplier network, and no single supplier or customer accounts for a material share of purchases or revenue, which keeps concentration risk low.

Key takeaway. Costco differentiates itself through its membership-driven revenue model, low-margin and high-volume strategy, and stable cash flows from renewal fees, while many peers rely more heavily on promotional pricing, discretionary spending, or higher merchandise margins. This gives Costco greater earnings visibility and more defensive characteristics than traditional retailers.

Industry outlook

Consumer staples retail is defensive: demand for necessities stays resilient in downturns, and large retailers benefit from economies of scale, supplier relationships, and logistics networks. The pressures are rising labor costs, supply chain inflation, thin margins, intense competition, and the investment that e-commerce and omnichannel retailing require. Success increasingly depends on scale, operational efficiency, and customer loyalty. Companies with disciplined cost structures and loyal customer bases, such as Costco, are well positioned to adapt.

02 Profitability analysis

The lowest margins in the group, and the most consistent returns.

Over fiscal years 2021 to 2025, Costco's profitability profile is defined by consistently low margins but very stable performance relative to Target, Walmart, and BJ's. Gross margin stays around 12 to 13% each year, clearly the lowest among peers, reflecting the low-markup, membership-driven strategy. Even with thinner gross margins, operating margin edges up from 3.47% to 3.77% and net margin rises from 2.56% to 2.94%, showing gradual improvement in operating efficiency.

Target's margins are more volatile, with a sharp drop in 2022 followed by partial recovery. Walmart is stable with moderate margins, and BJ's sits slightly above Costco on margins without improving as consistently. Costco ranks lowest on margins but strongest on consistency, an operational advantage in periods of inflation, supply chain pressure, and shifting consumer demand.

Return metrics are where Costco separates itself. ROA increases from 8.45% to about 10.50%, and RNOA holds at roughly 30 to 33%, indicating excellent operating asset productivity. ROE stays high and stable between about 25% and 31%.

DuPont analysis

ROE fluctuations were driven primarily by operational efficiency, not financial leverage. Net profit margin improved steadily from 2.56% to 2.94%. Total asset turnover increased from 3.41 to 3.75, reinforcing the high-volume model. The equity multiplier declined from 3.28 to 2.64, indicating reduced reliance on leverage. Despite lower leverage, ROE remained consistently high, which suggests shareholder returns are driven more by operating performance than by debt financing.

Costco has continued to show strong top-line growth driven by net merchandise sales and recurring membership fees, reflecting warehouse expansion, comparable sales growth, and sustained renewal rates. Cost of goods sold consistently represents about 87 to 88% of revenue, which points to disciplined pricing and operational efficiency as the drivers of growth, not margin expansion. BJ's also grew, but its growth has moderated more noticeably, and its COGS of about 81 to 82% of revenue reflects a different pricing structure.

Expense structure

Costco's cost structure is highly stable across five years. SG&A stays around 9% of revenue even through inflation and wage pressure, and operating income as a share of revenue is stable to slightly improving, which suggests operating leverage as revenue scales. BJ's carries SG&A of about 14 to 15% of revenue. It keeps higher gross margins, but the higher operating expense ratio indicates lower scale efficiency.

Five-year trend

Costco saw significant revenue growth immediately after the pandemic period, followed by normalization. Growth has moderated but remains positive, and operating income and net income have grown steadily. BJ's shows more variability, particularly in operating income and interest-related expenses.

Earnings quality

The ratio of operating cash flow to net income consistently exceeds 1.0 for Costco, indicating that reported earnings are supported by cash generation with low reliance on accruals. BJ's also exceeds 1.0 but with more year-to-year variability, suggesting higher sensitivity to working capital swings.

Where the revenue comes from

Revenue exposure is concentrated entirely within the warehouse clubs and superstores classification, making Costco a pure-play warehouse club retailer. Geographically, the United States accounts for about 72.7% of last-twelve-month revenue and Canada 13.4%, with smaller contributions from Mainland China, Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Spain, and South Korea. The Americas represent about 86 to 87% of revenue, and roughly 92% comes from developed markets. That reduces political and currency risk but limits exposure to high-growth emerging markets.

05 Debt analysis

Almost no leverage, and interest covered eighty times over.

12.89%Debt to total assets, 2025

86×+Operating cash flow to interest expense
> 3Altman Z-Score, safe zone all five years

Costco has maintained significantly lower leverage than its competitors over the past five years. In 2025 its interest-bearing debt is about 2.46% of the market value of equity and about 12.89% of total assets, the lowest among BJ's, Target, and Walmart. Costco finances a larger portion of its assets through equity and internal funding.

Coverage is extremely strong. EBITDA covers interest expense more than 80 times, and operating cash flow covers it more than 86 times, far above peers. The risk of financial distress from debt servicing is very low.

Altman Z-Score

The Z-Score has stayed well above the safe-zone threshold of 3 throughout the period. The strongest contributors are leverage and efficiency: market value of equity is very high relative to total liabilities, and Costco generates more than three dollars of sales for every dollar of assets. Liquidity looks weaker because of negative working capital, which is common among large retailers given rapid inventory turnover and favorable supplier terms.

Debt types and maturities

Long-term debt consists mainly of senior unsecured notes across various maturities, alongside lease liabilities for warehouses, distribution centers, and offices. The maturity schedule is staggered across multiple years, which reduces refinancing risk and preserves flexibility.

06 Cost of capital

A discount rate of 8.36%, built from CAPM.

8.36%WACC, base case
4.22%Risk-free rate, 10-year Treasury
5.0%Market risk premium
0.86Beta, average of FactSet and S&P Capital IQ

Costco's weighted average cost of capital is 8.36%, the weighted average of its cost of debt and cost of equity. It represents the required return for investors and serves as the discount rate in the valuation.

Cost of equity was estimated with the Capital Asset Pricing Model, using the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield of 4.22% as the risk-free rate and a market risk premium of 5.0%. Beta estimates came from FactSet and S&P Capital IQ, and their average of 0.86 was used as the base case. A lower beta produces a lower cost of equity and WACC, and a higher beta raises both. Cost of debt was calculated from market data and adjusted for taxes, and capital structure weights used the market value of equity and book value of debt.

07 Free cash flow valuation

The cash flows support $831.50 a share. The market was paying more.

The free cash flow model implies a value of $831.50 per share. Against a market price of about $994.68 when this section was written, that suggests Costco was overvalued by about $163 per share under the model's assumptions.

Reading the gap

The difference between the model value and the market price comes down to expectations. The market appears to price in stronger long-term growth, continued global expansion, and sustained success of the membership model. The FCF model is more conservative: it reduces growth gradually and builds in consistent reinvestment. Costco's fundamentals remain strong, but the market price reflects a premium to the intrinsic value estimated here.

08 Market multiples

Peers say $300 to $500. That is the premium, measured.

Per-share metrics for Costco and its peer group were taken from forward-looking FactSet estimates so that every comparable is on the same basis. Costco trades at a premium to peers, particularly on price-to-earnings and price-to-cash-flow, reflecting stronger profitability, operational efficiency, and its membership model.

Peer multiples were summarized by industry average, first quartile, and third quartile. Given Costco's financial performance and premium positioning, the third quartile was treated as the most appropriate benchmark. Even so, applying industry multiples to Costco's metrics implies a range of about $300 to $500 per share.

Key insight. Costco trades at a significant premium to its peers, one that traditional market multiples may not fully capture given its superior operating performance and market position.

09 Final recommendation

Hold and wait. A great company at a price that leaves no margin of safety.

Based on the valuation analysis, Costco appears overvalued relative to its intrinsic value estimate of $831.50 per share. With the market price at $970.31, that implies a downside of approximately 14.31%. Despite strong fundamentals and consistent financial performance, the valuation does not provide a sufficient margin of safety. The recommendation is to Hold / Wait, as investors may benefit from a more attractive entry point in the future.

Why the cash flow model carries all the weight

The FCF model produces $831.50 per share and serves as the basis for the overall estimate. Market-based multiples, including P/E, P/S, P/B, and P/CF, generate lower values of roughly $395 to $500 per share. Those relative models were excluded from the final estimate because they cannot fully capture Costco's operating performance, membership-based revenue, and long-term growth stability. The FCF model reflects intrinsic value from projected cash flows instead of market sentiment or peer comparison, so it received a 100% weighting.

Where this differs from the Street

Analyst consensus is more optimistic. The average price target is about $1,072, within a range of $650 to $1,315, and most analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, with Buy and Overweight ratings holding at roughly 55 to 65% over time and very few Sells.

This analysis arrives at a more conservative figure. Analysts often build forward-looking growth expectations, continued market dominance, and premium multiples into their targets. The FCF model emphasizes valuation discipline by focusing on underlying cash generation. The result is a Hold / Wait where the Street says Buy: even high-quality companies may not be attractive investments at elevated prices.

Final insight. Costco remains a high-quality, well-managed company with strong long-term fundamentals, but its market valuation limits upside. Investment decisions should be guided by disciplined entry pricing as well as company strength.
